### [SLIDE 4] The Gap Map

**TITLE:** Six Gaps. Three Blind Spots.

**BODY:**
**Lifecycle gaps:**
- Strategy & Vision — no competitive analysis or business model design upstream of discovery
- Delivery & Release — no release management, feature flagging, or progressive delivery

### [SLIDE 6] The Foundation

**TITLE:** Knowledge Engineering: Wave Zero

**BODY:**
- Every perimeter operates on knowledge. Most of it is trapped.
- In brownfield projects: tacit expertise, undocumented decisions, buried architectural rationale
- **Three capabilities:**
  - **Extract** — mine codebases, documentation, repository history
  - **Elicit** — structured conversations to externalize team expertise
  - **Curate** — maintain a knowledge graph; expose assets to every other perimeter
- Knowledge Engineering activates *first* — before discovery, before readiness assessment, before anything else

**SPEAKER NOTES:**
This is the wave zero argument. Without accessible knowledge, every subsequent perimeter operates partially blind. This is especially critical in brownfield — which is where most real engagements live.

### [SLIDE 8] The Organizational Dimension

**TITLE:** Tooling Is the Easy Part

**BODY:**
- AI agents are becoming **organizational participants** — not just tools
- Three emerging collaboration models (Harvard/Wharton research, 2024):
  - **Centaurs** — clear human/agent division of labor → domain upskilling
  - **Cyborgs** — fluid, integrated task blending → AI-native skill development
  - **Self-Automators** — delegation with minimal engagement → autonomy risk
- Adoption fails without: change architecture, governance design, psychological safety
- The **Governance & Change perimeter** governs the transformation itself — it runs from day one, not as an afterthought

**SPEAKER NOTES:**
Executives recognize this pattern: technology adoption that ignores organizational change fails. This slide shows awareness of that — and positions the governance perimeter as the answer.

### [SLIDE 21] The Knowledge Problem

**TITLE:** We Can Know More Than We Can Tell

**BODY:**
- Most organizational knowledge is tacit: architectural rationale, edge case expertise, tribal knowledge, experiment learnings
- Documentation captures what is *produced*. Knowledge Engineering extracts what *already exists but isn't accessible*.
- **Cognitive Debt** (Storey, 2026): as AI generates more code, human understanding of the codebase erodes — knowledge extraction becomes essential, not optional

**Key theoretical frames:**
- The Tacit Dimension (Polanyi, 1966) — "We can know more than we can tell"
- SECI Model (Nonaka & Takeuchi) — Externalization phase at scale
- Knowledge Elicitation (Cooke) — structured techniques for expert knowledge extraction
- GraphRAG (Microsoft Research, 2024) — graph-based retrieval over organizational knowledge
- Mining Software Repositories (Kagdi et al.) — the codebase as knowledge repository

### [SLIDE 23] The Domain Mesh Pattern

**TITLE:** Specialized Disciplines as Federated Capabilities

**BODY:**
- **The problem:** multiple technical disciplines (data, ML, agents, platform) can't be centralized without creating bottlenecks
- **The pattern:** domain-oriented ownership, capability as a product, self-serve mesh infrastructure, federated governance
- **Four domain modules proposed:** DataOps · MLOps · AgentOps · PlatformOps
- **Standard port interface (uniform across all domains):**
  - `assess(context)` → assessment artifact
  - `build(spec)` → implementation artifact
  - `monitor(target)` → monitoring configuration
  - `advise(question)` → domain-specific guidance

**Theoretical foundations:** Data Mesh (Dehghani) · Service Mesh (Istio/Linkerd) · Agentic Mesh (Broda, 2025) · Hexagonal Architecture (Cockburn) · Domain-Driven Design (Evans) · Composable Architecture (Gartner)

### [SLIDE 25] Change Management Frameworks

**TITLE:** Managing the Human Side of Agentic Adoption

**BODY:**
**Classical change management:**
- Kotter's 8-Step Model — create urgency → build coalition → form vision → sustain acceleration
- Lewin's Model — Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze

- Bridges' Transition Model — Ending · Neutral Zone · New Beginning
- McKinsey 7-S — Structure, Strategy, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, Shared Values

**Organizational design for agents:**
- Team Topologies (Skelton & Pais) — stream-aligned, enabling, platform, complicated-subsystem teams
- unFIX Model (Appelo) — organizational design iteration alongside agent capability evolution
- Conway's Law & Inverse Conway Maneuver — restructure teams to produce desired agent architectures
- Sociotechnical Systems Theory (Trist & Bamforth) — joint optimization of social and technical subsystems

**Governance:**
- Cynefin Framework (Snowden) — which decisions agents handle vs. require human judgment
- TOGAF — structured governance for technology decisions including AI agent architecture
- IEEE 7000 Series — accountability, transparency, responsible AI
- Dynamic Capabilities (Teece et al.) — organizational capacity to continuously evolve agent deployment

### [SLIDE 27] Open Questions & Research Agenda

**TITLE:** What We Don't Know Yet

**BODY:**
1. **Agent-to-agent governance** — how should inter-module handoff contracts be governed across teams? (Data Mesh federated governance offers a starting point)
2. **Emergent behavior at scale** — with 40+ agents across 15+ modules, what guardrails are needed?
3. **Measuring transformation** — DORA covers delivery; what metrics capture discovery quality, knowledge asset health, adoption success, governance maturity?
4. **Agent autonomy spectrum** — how does an organization safely increase agent delegation over time?
5. **Convoke's own governance** — as a three-axis platform, how should the project itself be governed?
6. **Knowledge asset lifecycle** — how do knowledge assets age, decay, and retire? (Storey's cognitive debt is increasingly urgent)
7. **Port interface evolution** — how do domain module interfaces evolve without breaking lifecycle consumers? (Semver, consumer-driven contracts)
8. **Mesh observability** — how do we observe which domain capabilities are most consumed, where bottlenecks are?

**SPEAKER NOTES:**
These questions are the research agenda. They're not blockers — they're the inputs to the next iteration. Some will be answered through Team Factory specifications; others require dedicated architectural spikes.
